Harsh & Holy

a week in ladakh · july 20221

A sky full of stars had been a concept, a song even.

Here, they were real.

Everything else — the cold, the road, the air or lack thereof — was the price of admission.

"Of immovable things, I am the Himalayas."

The Gita says it simply. Below the plane window,2 for the first time, you believe it.

For most of the group, this was a first time. I'd been before. I remembered a little of what was coming. Not enough.

The land above eleven thousand feet
is a different country.
Grass stops trying. Trees surrender.

What's left is colour — umber, ash, bone, rust — and the wind that moves between them.

You notice your breath first.3 Then your heart. Then a headache behind the eyes that nobody warned you about.4

We were climbing toward Khardung La5 — one of the highest motorable passes in the world — when the clouds lowered onto the road. Someone asked if it was rain.

I said no.

They stepped out. Some of them wept.

Nobody spoke.

At 18,000 feet, a handful of steps earns you breathlessness. The cold doesn't knock8 — it enters, takes the warmth from your hands first, then your face. And still they stood in it, arms wide, faces up. One of them was mouthing something I couldn't hear. I think she was thanking it.

I woke them
at two.

Nubra is what the sky does when nothing is in the way.6

It took maybe a minute for our eyes to adjust. Then it appeared.

I didn't yet know how to photograph stars. The sky I've shown you is from a different valley, a different night. But it is the sky we saw.

  • The Milky Way — a band of light we had only seen on laptop wallpapers.
  • Jupiter.
  • A satellite, crossing.

Not all rivers shout.

The Zanskar here is slow, brown, enormous.7 It does not roar. It exhales. You can watch it for a long time and understand why people once decided the oldest gods lived in water.

Then one of us — quietly, almost to herself — started to sing.

Chamba kitni door…

Her voice was not a performance. She wasn't singing for us; she was singing at the river, the way you might speak to someone you hadn't seen in a long time.

The mountains listened. The dog listened. We listened.

That was the second silence of the trip. The first had been under the stars.

We came back
smaller.

Best Season

We went in July — ideal for high passes. June to early September is the main window. After September, Khardung La and most passes begin closing for winter. Peak season means more crowds but more open roads.

Temperatures in July

  • Leh valley (3,500m) — 25–30°C day · 10–15°C night
  • Khardung La (5,359m) — near 0°C · snowfall even in July
  • Nubra Valley — warmer and more sheltered than Leh
  • Pangong Tso (4,350m) — cold, windy, intense sun · extreme UV

What to Wear

  • Thermals — top and bottom, for nights and high passes
  • Fleece mid-layer
  • Windproof and waterproof outer jacket
  • Gloves and a warm hat — non-optional above 14,000ft
  • UV-protective sunglasses — altitude means stronger UV
  • SPF 50+ sunscreen — you burn faster than you think
  • Light layers for warm Leh valley days

Key Altitudes

  • Leh — 3,500m · your base · acclimatise here first
  • Khardung La — 5,359m · one of the highest motorable passes in the world
  • Nubra Valley — 3,048m · lower, easier to breathe
  • Pangong Tso — 4,350m · cold, dry, and remote

Acclimatisation

Spend at least two full days in Leh before going higher. The body needs time to adjust. Fit and young is no defence against AMS. Don't rush this.

Several people in our group got sick despite preparation. There is no guarantee. Some bodies adjust; others don't. Go slowly.

Altitude Kit

  • Diamox (acetazolamide) — consult a doctor before travelling
  • Portable oxygen cans — available in Leh markets
  • Cloves — chewing relieves altitude headache
  • Camphor (kapur) — inhaling helps with breathlessness
  • Chewing gum — helps with ear pressure at high passes
  • Motion sickness tablets — mountain roads are relentless

Pangong — No Overnight Stays

Do not stay overnight at Pangong Tso. At 4,350m, with cold nights and no nearby medical facility, it is genuinely risky. Overnight stays at the lake are also not permitted. Day trip only.

Vegetarian

  • Thukpa — noodle soup. The staple. Every kitchen does it differently.
  • Skyu — thick pasta stew with root vegetables. Hearty mountain food.
  • Momos — steamed dumplings. Available everywhere, endlessly variable.
  • Butter tea (Po cha) — salty, yak-buttered. An acquired taste worth acquiring.
  • Khambir — local sourdough bread, best with apricot jam.
  • Tsampa — roasted barley flour, eaten as porridge or mixed into tea.
  • Apricot products — Ladakh's apricots are exceptional. Jam, oil, dried fruit.

Non-Vegetarian

  • Mutton thukpa — richer and more warming at altitude than the veg version.
  • Mutton momos — the standard. Ask for the local version, not the tourist menu.
  • Yak meat — seasonal, not always available. Try if you see it.
  • Timouk — Ladakhi spiced meat stew, slow-cooked.
  • Chhurpe — dried yak cheese, very hard. Good for the road.

Eating at Altitude

Your appetite often drops at high altitude. Eat anyway — the body needs fuel. For the first day or two in Leh, stick to soups and light food. Avoid heavy meals and alcohol while acclimatising.

Pashmina

The real thing comes from Changthangi goats on the Changthang plateau. Genuinely fine, soft, and warm. The Leh market has both authentic and imitation — price difference is significant.

Burn test: genuine Pashmina burns like hair. Synthetics melt. Buy from certified shops, not first stalls.

Shilajit

A dark resin from Himalayan rocks, used in Ayurvedic medicine for centuries — traditionally for energy, immunity, and altitude adaptation. Buy from pharmacies or certified shops only.

Purified shilajit only. Raw or unpurified shilajit may contain heavy metals.

Thangka Paintings

Traditional Buddhist scroll paintings on cloth. A good hand-painted thangka takes weeks. Machine-printed vs. hand-painted is easy to distinguish once you look closely.

Apricots & Local Produce

Sun-dried apricots, apricot oil, apricot kernels. Also look for sea buckthorn — the local superfruit, high in vitamins. Both available across Leh market.

Prayer Flags & Handicrafts

Lung ta (prayer flags), hand-woven wool carpets, Ladakhi jewellery (turquoise, coral, silver). Monastery shops often carry locally made pieces at honest prices.
